 **Landtify**

**A Regenerative Land Governance Solution**

**The Reality We Are Addressing (Ground Truth)**

In the Tamale Metropolitan Area and many parts of Northern Ghana, land ownership is governed through traditional authority systems, sub-divisional chiefs, paramount chiefs, family heads, and community land administrators. These systems are culturally legitimate and widely respected, but they rely almost entirely on manual, paper-based processes.

What is done currently is that:

·       Chiefs secretaries keep mapped copy of all lands to be allocated.

·       Allocation letters are handwritten in printed templates.

·       Chiefs’ secretaries store records in filing cabinets or notebooks.

·       Buyers walk away with loose documents that can be lost, altered, or duplicated.

There is no central registry at the community level. No shared record that confirms whether a parcel has already been allocated, transferred, or disputed.

As a result:

- If is common for the same plot of land is often sold multiple times
- Verification depends on memory, trust, or personal relationships
- Over 70% of land is never registered with the Lands Commission
- Disputes escalate into court cases, family conflicts, and community tension

The people most affected include:

- First-time land buyers and families building homes
- Chiefs and their secretaries, whose credibility suffers
- Surveyors and land administrators
- Real estate developers and investors

This reality weakens trust, slows development, and creates persistent conflict.  
This is the ground truth Landtify is responding to.

**Insights – What We Learned**

As we studied these challenges across communities, several powerful insights emerged.

**What is consistent everywhere?**

Every community faces the same core issue – no shared, verifiable, digital record of land ownership.

**What varies?**

Some chiefs keep better handwritten records than others, but even the most organized systems cannot prevent duplication, because they are isolated and offline.

**What surprised us?**

Most chiefs, secretaries, surveyors, and buyers are not resistant to transparency or technology.  
The real barrier is the absence of a tool that respects traditional authority while solving verification and record-keeping problems.

**The core insight**

The problem is not that chiefs are intentionally issuing fraudulent land. The problem is that there is no trusted, shared system to track issuance, verify ownership, and record transfers.

This absence of verification breaks trust, fuels disputes, and weakens the land ecosystem.

Land governance does not need to be replaced; it needs to be strengthened with the right technology.

**Hypothesis – What We Believe Can Work**

From these insights, we formed a clear set of hypotheses that guide Landtify:

- If each land parcel is represented as a unique digital asset **(NFT)**, double-selling will become nearly impossible.
- If chiefs and secretaries issue digital allocation records, trust in land transactions will increase.
- If buyers can verify a parcel before payment, fraud and disputes will significantly reduce.
- If ownership history is stored on blockchain, records will be tamper-proof, transparent, and permanent.
- If the system is simple, mobile-friendly, and aligned with existing workflows, traditional authorities will adopt it without advanced technical skills.

**Our central hypothesis**

Digitizing community land records on blockchain will reassure trust, reduce conflict, and create a transparent land ecosystem that supports sustainable development and future government integration.

**The Landtify Solution (What We Are Building)**

**Landtify** is a blockchain-backed community land registry and verification platform designed specifically for traditional land governance systems.

**How it works**

- Each land parcel is digitized and represented as a **unique NFT-like asset**
- The asset includes:

- GPS coordinates / site plan
- Allocation details
- Ownership history
- Issuing authority (chief / palace)

- Chiefs or secretaries issue and manage allocations digitally
- Buyers can verify authenticity before purchasing
- Transfers of ownership are recorded transparently on the blockchain

Landtify does not replace chiefs.  
It empowers them with modern tools to protect their authority and their communities.

**Final Summary — The Landtify Story**

- **Ground Truth:** Land fraud persists because land records are manual, fragmented, and unverifiable.
- **Insights:** The real challenge is the absence of a shared, trusted registry connecting tradition and verification.
- **Hypotheses:** Blockchain-based digital land assets can prevent duplication, secure ownership, and rebuild trust.
- **Opportunities:** Prototype, pilot, hackathon impact, and long-term integration with national land systems.

Landtify is a bridge between tradition and technology, trust and transparency, today’s realities and tomorrow’s land systems.
