**Community: Fuo, Tamale Metropolitan Area, Northern Region, Ghana**

 **GROUND TRUTHS (Facts & Events – Past)**

Fuo is a growing community on the outskirts of Tamale with poor drainage systems, unplanned settlement expansion, and clay-heavy soil that does not absorb water quickly. During the rainy season, residents experience **frequent flash floods**, especially after sudden overnight storms.

Through community visits, interviews, and observation, several patterns emerged:

- **Residents depend on word-of-mouth alerts**—often from neighbours shouting or calling when water levels rise unexpectedly.
- **There is no structured flood reporting system**, meaning information travels late, causing people to lose livestock, food items, learning materials, and occasionally suffer injuries.
- **Mobile phones are common**, but residents lack an organised digital tool to report rising water levels or receive early warnings.
- Local health facilities report **increases in malaria, diarrhoea, and contaminated-water diseases** after flood events.
- Small business owners—especially women trading foodstuffs—experience **frequent financial losses** when their stores or stock get soaked.
- Youth in the community expressed frustration that they “**see the flood coming but don’t know who to tell or what to do**.”

These ground truths show a community experiencing preventable harm simply because **information fails to reach the right people at the right time**.
