**. Ground Truth (What We Saw and Heard on the Ground)**
Tamale is a very large city with both urban and rural combined features. in Tamale we identified 
so many areas but the most prominent areas we visited When we moved through different parts of Tamale were communities known as (Choggu, Lamashegu, Jisonayili, Vitting, Sakasaka)—we kept hearing the same complaint from almost everyone:
**“Finding a room now is too stressful.”**
During our conversations, people shared experiences and this is a summary below to explain how deep the situation is really affecting them. 
- _“I had to walk from community to community just to check if someone had a room for rent.”_
- _“Landlords post rooms on WhatsApp or TikTok, but the information is scattered.”_
- _“Agents charge too much and sometimes show the same room to ten people.”_
Landlords and caretakers also expressed frustration:
- _“We have rooms, but many people don’t know.”_
- _“Sometimes a room stays vacant for months simply because people cannot find it.”_
We quickly realized that **the problem isn’t lack of rooms but is rather lack of access to reliable information
And this affects everyone ranging from students, families, workers, newcomers, businesses, and even landlords themselves. 

**2. Insights (Thinking From What the Community Told Us)**
From the discussions, we noticed something interesting, we realised the problem is not new. People have always searched manually for rooms. But the population has grown, and the stress has grown with it.
secondly we realized that **Technology exists—but not in the right form** . People are using WhatsApp, TikTok, Facebook, and random posters…  
but none of these give **organized, verified, location-based** rental information
We also realized that **. Landlords want tenants, tenants want rooms — but they are not connecting that means the gap between supply and demand is not technical**,
it is **organizational and information-based**. 
again we realized the people most affected are the most vulnerable.  people such as - New migrants, Students, Low-income earners, Rural-urban movers, People without smartphones
this was mentioned by one of the women in that community. 
She said, i cote: _“I spent two weeks walking around. Transport alone cost me more than the agent fee.”_
**3. Hypotheses (What We Think We Can Improve Based on the Conversations)** 
After talking to many groups—landlords, caretakers, youth, students, agents—we formed several beliefs:

**1. If we put rental information in one place, stress will reduce drastically.**

**2. If landlords can upload vacancies easily, rooms won’t remain empty unnecessarily.**

**3. If tenants can check location, price, and availability before moving around, they’ll save money, time, and energy.**

**4. A USSD system will help people who don’t have smartphones.**

**5. Transparency will reduce agent exploitation and price inflation.**

**6. Anonymous complaints or reports could support fair housing practices.**

These are the ideas shaping our direction.

**4. Opportunities (What The Community Helped Us See for the Future)**

From these conversations, we recognized several opportunities:

**1. A central rental marketplace for Tamale**

Where landlords, caretakers, and tenants meet digitally.

**2. Verified listings to reduce fraud**

So people stop wasting time on “fake” rooms.

**3. Location filters based on community names**

People can search by the exact area they want to live.

**4. USSD support**

To make the system available to low-income groups and rural migrants.

**5. Community-based updating**

Caretakers and youth groups can help keep listings current.

**6. Data for better urban planning**

With real numbers, city authorities can understand housing pressure points.

These opportunities came directly from community voices.
